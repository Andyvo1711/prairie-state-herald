The Illinois State Board of Education released its 2026 State Report Card on Oct. 30, and Chalkbeat Chicago highlighted three trends: more top-rated schools, modest proficiency gains and falling but still elevated absenteeism.

ISBE changed how it rates schools earlier this year, sorting them into five categories from Exemplary to Comprehensive. Under the old system, only the top 10% of schools could be Exemplary. State Superintendent Tony Sanders said that approach could leave a school that improved significantly "kicked out of the Exemplary band." Under the new setup, 27% of schools statewide are Exemplary, up 17%, while nearly three-quarters were Commendable last year.

In Chicago, the share of Exemplary schools rose from 2% in 2025 to 9% in 2026. The share of CPS schools in the two lowest categories also grew. Annise Lewis, CPS deputy chief education officer, said the ratings are not "the end-all be-all" and that the district weighs academic outcomes with student growth and well-being.

Test results improved. About 56% of Illinois students are proficient in English language arts, up from 53%, and almost 40% in math, up from 38.5%. Because the state lowered proficiency cut scores last year, earlier years are not directly comparable. In Chicago, ELA proficiency rose 3 points to nearly 46%, while math proficiency for grades 3-8 remains more than 15 points below ELA.

Chronic absenteeism has declined four years in a row but remains above pre-2020 levels. Outside Chicago, 23% of students were chronically absent. CPS peaked near 45% in 2022 and has since dropped about 6 points. Gaps persist: 44% of Black students were chronically absent compared with 23% of white students.

Other figures from the report card: the statewide four-year graduation rate is nearly 89%, Illinois has nearly 144,000 teachers, and teacher retention is above 90% for the first time in more than a decade.

*Source: Chalkbeat Chicago, Oct. 30, 2026.*
