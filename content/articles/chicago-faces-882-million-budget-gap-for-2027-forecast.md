Chicago is projecting an $882 million budget gap for 2027, according to the city's newly released financial forecast, reported by FOX 32 Chicago on Sept. 3.

The figure is down from the roughly $1.2 billion gap forecast for the same year a year ago. Mayor Brandon Johnson said the gap is nearly $284 million lower than last year's projection and credited revenue measures that have outperformed expectations.

## Revenue bright spots

City officials said one of the largest contributors is the Personal Property Lease Transaction Tax, which is projected to bring in about $41 million more than expected.

## Rising costs

Several expenses are pushing in the other direction. The forecast includes a $266 million increase in settlements and judgments compared with 2026, with the city budgeting about $401 million for them next year. Officials said the costs include expected payouts from cases dating back decades. Debt service is expected to rise by about $70 million, and the city anticipates higher costs for fuel, energy, employee benefits and pensions.

The forecast assumes Chicago will make its full supplemental pension payment in 2027. Officials said they plan to make the second half of this year's supplemental payment in September, bringing supplemental payments to the four city pension funds to about $1 billion since 2023.

## No answer yet on how to close it

Asked whether he would propose a property tax increase, Johnson did not rule one out. He said he wants "those who have the ability to contribute more to do more." He also declined to commit to bringing back the head tax that drew opposition from City Council members last year.

On possible layoffs, Johnson gave no specific plan but said protecting city workers and critical services remains his priority. He said his full budget proposal would come in the coming weeks.

## Open questions

A major variable is the surplus from tax increment financing districts. City officials currently estimate it at $330 million to $340 million, but said the number could change before the final budget is presented. That matters for Chicago Public Schools, which is relying on hundreds of millions of dollars from a TIF surplus in its own budget.

Members of the City Council's Budget Accountability Coalition issued a statement criticizing the administration's handling of revenue measures approved last year.
