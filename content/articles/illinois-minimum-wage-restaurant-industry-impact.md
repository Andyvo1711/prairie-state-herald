Illinois restaurant owners are adjusting menu prices and staffing levels following the latest scheduled increase in the state's minimum wage, part of a multi-year phase-in that has steadily raised the wage floor for hourly workers across the state's restaurant and retail industries.

Restaurant industry representatives said the increase has hit smaller, independently owned restaurants harder than larger chains, which generally have more flexibility to absorb labor cost increases across a larger revenue base or through automation investments that smaller operators often cannot afford.

"A restaurant with fifteen employees doesn't have the same options a chain with a thousand locations has for spreading out this kind of cost increase," said the head of a state restaurant industry association. "A lot of our members are looking at menu price increases in the range of five to eight percent just to maintain the margins they had before this latest increase."

Some restaurant owners said they have also reduced staffing levels or scaled back hours for certain positions in response to the higher wage floor, while others said they have maintained staffing but passed the cost increase through to customers via higher prices. Economists studying the state's minimum wage phase-in have offered mixed assessments of its overall employment impact, with some research showing modest job losses concentrated among the lowest-margin restaurants and other research finding limited measurable effect on overall industry employment.

Restaurant owners in Chicago said the combination of the state minimum wage increase and the city's own elevated labor costs has made the market particularly challenging for small independent operators compared with restaurants in suburban and downstate areas. Restaurant industry representatives said they expect continued price increases across the sector as the wage schedule completes its final phases.
