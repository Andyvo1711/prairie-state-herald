West Nile virus has arrived in earnest in Illinois, with the state reporting its first human case of the year in late August and Chicago announcing a death a few days later.

The Illinois Department of Public Health confirmed on August 26 that a Piatt County resident was the state's first person known to have been infected in 2026, according to station KWQC. Three additional cases were confirmed in northern Illinois. IDPH said the virus had by then been detected in 50 Illinois counties through mosquitoes, horses or humans.

On September 1, the Chicago Department of Public Health reported the city's first two human cases of the season, including the first death linked to the virus in 2026, according to FOX 32 Chicago. The department said IDPH had also confirmed several more cases elsewhere in the state.

CDPH Commissioner Garth Walker pointed to the weather. "2026 has seen the rainiest summer in Chicago in years," he said in a statement, noting that the wet conditions gave mosquitoes plenty of places to breed.

West Nile is the most common mosquito-borne disease in Chicago and across the United States. It spreads through the bite of an infected mosquito and is not typically passed from person to person. Most infected people never develop symptoms, but about one in five get a fever and flu-like illness. Severe illness occurs in roughly one in 150 cases and is most likely in people over 60 and those with chronic medical conditions.

Health officials urge residents to follow what IDPH calls the three Rs. To reduce mosquito exposure, keep screens on windows and doors tight and repair tears. To repel, wear long sleeves, long pants, socks and closed shoes outdoors and use an EPA-registered repellent as directed. To report, tell local authorities about spots where water has pooled for more than seven days, because standing water is where mosquitoes breed.
