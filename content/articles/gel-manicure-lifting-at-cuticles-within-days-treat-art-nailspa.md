A recent review of TREAT&ART NailSpa comes from a customer whose gel manicure began lifting at the cuticles within about a week of her appointment. Her review is short and direct, and it points to one of the most common reasons clients feel let down by a gel service: how long the manicure lasts. Because this is a topic every nail team deals with, it makes a very practical case to learn from.

## Preparation Is What Helps Gel Hold On

The customer explained what she believed went wrong. In her observation, the technician did not buff or file any of her natural nail, so the gel polish "had no surface to latch onto." Whether or not that was the full picture, her comment highlights a step that clients can often see and feel for themselves. Gel adheres best to a nail that has been properly prepared, and many clients now know that a light buff, a clean cuticle area, and a dry surface are part of a good service.

A reliable prep routine is easy to teach and easy to repeat. It usually includes gently pushing back and cleaning the cuticle area, lightly buffing the shine off the natural nail, removing dust and oils, and applying the base coat in a thin layer that stays just clear of the skin. Lifting at the cuticle can also be linked to product touching the skin or to a thick layer near the cuticle, so the technician's brush control at that edge matters as well. Finally, each layer needs to cure fully under the lamp for the length of time the product recommends.

## Letting the Client See the Care

Clients cannot always tell which steps are happening, but they can tell when they are skipped or happen too quickly. A technician can build confidence by saying a few words as the service moves along, such as "I am going to lightly buff the surface now so the gel lasts longer." This takes a few seconds and shows the client that the long-term result is part of the plan. It also gives clients the chance to ask questions if their nails are thin or sensitive, so the technician can adjust the approach.

## A Clear Aftercare Message and a Simple Repair Policy

Since the manicure did not last a full week, the customer ended her review by saying she would not be coming back. That is often the quiet outcome when a client feels the service did not meet expectations: she does not return for a repair, she simply moves on. A short and friendly aftercare note at checkout can help prevent this, for example, how to care for the cuticles, what to avoid in the first day or two, and an invitation to come back if any lifting appears.

A simple repair window, such as a free fix if lifting occurs within a stated number of days, gives staff a clear script and gives clients a reason to return. It turns a disappointing moment into a chance to show that the salon stands behind its work.

## Editorial Perspective

This case is a reminder that a gel manicure is judged in the days after the appointment, not only when the client leaves the chair. Three practical lessons stand out.

First, treat nail preparation as a consistent routine, including a light buff, a clean cuticle area, and a thin, careful application near the skin. Second, explain key steps out loud so clients can see the care that goes into a lasting result. Third, offer clear aftercare guidance and a simple repair policy so that early lifting leads to a return visit rather than a lost client.

When prep, communication, and follow-up work together, gel manicures last longer and clients feel confident coming back.
