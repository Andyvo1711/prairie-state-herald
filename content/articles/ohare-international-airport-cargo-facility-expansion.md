O'Hare International Airport broke ground this week on a new air cargo facility, a $70 million investment aimed at accommodating rising e-commerce shipping volume that has increasingly flowed through the airport's cargo operations, already among the busiest in the country.

The new 230,000-square-foot facility will meaningfully expand the airport's existing cargo handling capacity, airport officials said, addressing bottlenecks that have emerged as several major shipping carriers have expanded operations at O'Hare to serve the growing Midwest e-commerce distribution market.

"Cargo volume through this airport has grown faster than our physical infrastructure for handling it, and that's created real operational strain during peak shipping periods," said the airport authority's chief of cargo operations. "This facility is designed to get ahead of that demand rather than continuing to play catch-up."

The project is funded through a combination of airport revenue bonds and a federal infrastructure grant, with construction expected to take approximately 20 months. Airport officials said the expanded facility is expected to support several hundred new jobs in cargo handling, sorting, and logistics coordination once fully operational.

O'Hare's cargo growth reflects the broader regional trend of e-commerce distribution facilities concentrating around Chicago, drawn by the area's central geography and dense rail and highway infrastructure. Airport officials said they expect cargo volume to continue growing even after the new facility opens, and are already evaluating sites for potential further expansion within the next decade.
