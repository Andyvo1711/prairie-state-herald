OSF HealthCare unveiled a new mobile primary care clinic in Rockford on Tuesday, aiming to bring continuing medical care to neighborhoods and rural areas where it is hard to find, the Rock River Current reported.

The OSF OnCall Primary Care on the Go van was introduced at a news conference and blessing led by Bishop David Malloy at St. Bernadette Church on the city's west side, which will serve as one of its regular stops. Other scheduled stops include St. Elizabeth Adult Center on the southwest side and Patriots Gateway Community Center in Midtown, along with rural locations that lack a nearby clinic.

OSF executives said the van serves two purposes: reaching patients now and helping the system judge where a permanent clinic might eventually be needed. "We're going into this new fiscal year really around how do we grow and how do we bring care closer to the communities we serve," said Alec Grabowski, president of OSF HealthCare Saint Anthony Medical Center.

Kate Barth, vice president of OnDemand Services for OSF OnCall, stressed that the van is meant to work like a standing practice rather than a one-day health fair. Patients can build a relationship with the same provider and receive preventive care, chronic disease management, wellness visits and treatment for common illnesses. The van also offers lab draws, point-of-care testing, electrocardiograms and virtual care when appropriate.

Appointments follow the same scheduling process as OSF's brick-and-mortar clinics, and patients are billed the same way, with no difference in co-pays. Stops, schedules and openings will be posted through OSF MyChart and the OSF OnCall website once service begins later in August.

Michelle Warning, an advanced practice registered nurse, will regularly provide care on the van. OSF also told the Current it plans to partner with employers in the future to bring the service closer to workplaces.
