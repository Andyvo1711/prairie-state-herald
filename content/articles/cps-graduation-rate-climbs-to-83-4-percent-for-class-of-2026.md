Chicago Public Schools says 83.4% of its students graduated on time in 2026, up from 82.5% for the class of 2025. District officials announced the figure on Oct. 21, according to Chalkbeat Chicago.

It is the second consecutive year of improvement after a dip for the class of 2024. Over the longer run, the rate has climbed by almost 10 percentage points since 2018, though with setbacks along the way, particularly after the pandemic.

CEO Macquline King presented the numbers at Von Steuben Metropolitan Science Center in North Park, joined by her leadership team and an audience of students and educators. She also pointed to college enrollment: about seven in 10 CPS graduates from the class of 2025, the most recent data available, enrolled in college. Chalkbeat noted that figure compares with roughly five in 10 at high-poverty schools nationally, per the National Student Clearinghouse Research Center, and trails the rate at low-poverty schools by a few points.

The district also highlighted sharp recent growth in students who graduate with college credit through dual-credit courses. Chalkbeat reported earlier that the results have been mixed, with more dual-credit participation at some schools not translating into higher college enrollment or persistence.

Attendance remains a concern. Chronic absenteeism, meaning students missing more than 10% of school days, rose after the pandemic and has hovered around 40%. A Chalkbeat and WBEZ investigation last year found district officials had been giving students more chances to make up missed work or classes.

The host school illustrates the picture. Von Steuben saw more than 96% of students graduate on time in 2025, and CPS said the figure dipped to 93.7% for the class of 2026. Principal Jennifer Sutton said the school ties attendance to incentives: students need 90% attendance to take a dual-credit course, and good attendance to attend homecoming or prom. Seniors meet with college counselors at least three times a year.

CPS had not released school-by-school graduation rates when Chalkbeat published its report.
