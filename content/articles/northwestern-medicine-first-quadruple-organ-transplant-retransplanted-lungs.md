Surgeons at Northwestern Medicine in Chicago have performed what the health system describes as the first known quadruple-organ transplant in the United States that involved retransplanted lungs, according to coverage published in mid-July.

The patient, Elizabeth Wehrle, is a 36-year-old mother from Montezuma, Iowa, who has cystic fibrosis. She received a double lung transplant in 2017, but became ill in January and was diagnosed in late February 2026 with a severe form of chronic rejection, KCRG-TV reported.

Northwestern physicians said her condition was a rare form of rejection called restrictive allograft syndrome. Dr. Ankit Bharat, chief of thoracic surgery at Northwestern Medicine, said it is often fatal and so high-risk that many transplant centers would not consider a repeat lung transplant.

The case grew more complicated. Doctors discovered that complications of cystic fibrosis had also damaged her liver and kidney, meaning she would need three organ types replaced. By then she was on a ventilator and ECMO, a machine that supports the heart and lungs, and her team in Iowa referred her to Chicago.

Wehrle was admitted to the cardiothoracic intensive care unit at Northwestern Memorial Hospital and listed for the combined transplant on March 19. Three days later, a donor and the donor's family made four organs available.

Thoracic surgeons began the lung retransplant on the evening of March 22, and abdominal surgeons followed with the liver and kidney in the early hours of March 23. Dr. Chitaru Kurihara, surgical director of the lung transplant program at the Canning Thoracic Institute, said splitting the work between two teams brought the total operating time to about eight hours, rather than a single 20-hour procedure that would have strained a critically ill patient.

Bharat said the earlier surgery had left extensive scar tissue that distorted the normal anatomy of her chest, making a second lung operation extraordinarily difficult. Dr. Satish Nadig, director of the Northwestern Medicine Comprehensive Transplant Center, credited a coordinated multidisciplinary team.

Wehrle said her first transplant had given her life back as a stay-at-home mother who homeschooled her son, before her health declined and everyday tasks became overwhelming. The July reports describe the operation as a success.
