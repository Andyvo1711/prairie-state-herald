When Illinois lawmakers pass rules on cosmetics, the people who handle those products every day are often the first to react. In Carbondale, one salon owner says the state's new ingredient law largely confirms a direction her profession was already heading, according to a report from KFVS12 Heartland News published August 26, 2026.

## A law that goes past federal rules

KFVS reported that Illinois is adding regulation of cosmetic ingredients under the Chemicals in Cosmetic Products Act, going beyond federal requirements. The law, signed by Gov. J.B. Pritzker, applies to makeup, hair, nail and tanning products, soaps and lotions, and perfumes and colognes. Among the regulated chemicals are formaldehyde and mercury.

The Illinois Policy Institute has reported that the sales restrictions begin July 1, 2028, and apply to ingredients already banned in the European Union.

## A salon's vetting process

Elizabeth Strusz, founder of E2 Salon in Carbondale, told KFVS that her business has long held high standards for the products it uses. When she considers bringing in a new product line, she said, she asks the company what ingredients it has removed over the past five years and where its education comes from.

She said she wants to be sure anything offered to clients is of the highest grade, a standard she described as already part of how her salon operates.

## Cleaner beauty becomes mainstream

Strusz told the station that the beauty industry has seen calls to move toward cleaner ingredients and to give both professionals and consumers a better understanding of what is in their products. In her view, the new rules are in some ways catching up with that shift.

She also credited social media, including TikTok, with making consumers more aware of what they put on their skin, the report said.

## What it means for small salons

For independent salons outside the Chicago area, the change is likely to show up through suppliers rather than inspections: the law is directed at companies that make, sell or hold products for sale. Owners such as Strusz, who already question vendors about formulas, may find the transition easier than shops that stock a wide range of discount lines.

KFVS did not report on enforcement details, and the law's start date is still nearly two years away.
