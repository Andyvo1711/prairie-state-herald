High school students in Chicago's western suburbs can now start training for a beauty career before they graduate. Tricoci University of Beauty Culture announced on September 30, 2026, that it has entered a new articulation agreement with Proviso Township District 209.

## How the program works

According to the company's announcement, the Vocational-Technical Contract for Instructional Program in Cosmetology was finalized on August 1, 2026, and took effect August 17, 2026. It is open to eligible juniors and seniors in District 209 and is delivered through the university's Chicago Northwest campus.

The two-year program gives students instruction and hands-on training from licensed beauty professionals in cosmetology and barbering. Participants earn high school credit and also receive credit toward licensing requirements, while being introduced to career options in the industry.

## A first for the school

Tricoci describes the arrangement as its first-ever articulation agreement. The term refers to a formal arrangement in which coursework taken at one institution counts toward requirements at another.

Larry Foster, the university's chief operating officer, said in the release that career readiness means giving students the chance to explore interests and build real-world skills before graduation, and he framed the partnership in those terms.

## Why it matters for the industry

The agreement lands as Illinois prepares to open a second route into the beauty trades. A law signed in June, House Bill 3460, creates a paid apprenticeship option for barbers, cosmetologists, estheticians, nail technicians and hair braiders beginning in 2027, according to the Illinois Policy Institute. Until now, aspiring licensees in the state have largely had to complete classroom hours at a school.

Programs that start in high school could shorten the path for students who know early that they want to work in a salon or barbershop, though the release does not say how many hours of licensing credit a student can accumulate.

## What is not yet known

The announcement does not state how many students are expected to enroll, what the program costs families, or how many licensing hours it counts toward. Eligibility is described only as being for qualified juniors and seniors within the district.
