CME Group, the Chicago-based derivatives marketplace, reported record trading volume last quarter, according to figures released by the exchange, reinforcing the city's long-standing status as one of the world's leading centers for futures and options trading.

Exchange officials said average daily trading volume climbed significantly compared with the prior year, driven by continued growth in interest rate futures and heightened volatility across commodity and equity index products that has pushed more institutional traders to hedge positions actively.

"Volatility is good business for an exchange, and we've seen sustained elevated volatility across multiple asset classes for a while now," said CME Group's chief market economist. "That's translated directly into higher trading volumes across nearly every major product line we offer."

The exchange's continued growth has supported hiring across its Chicago headquarters, where CME Group remains one of the city's largest financial sector employers. Exchange officials said the company has also continued investing in its electronic trading infrastructure, reducing its reliance on the open-outcry trading floor that once defined the exchange's public image.

CME Group said the record volume supports thousands of jobs directly tied to trading, technology, and clearing operations in Chicago, along with a broader ecosystem of trading firms, quantitative research shops, and financial technology companies that have clustered around the exchange over decades. City economic development officials have continued to emphasize the exchange's presence as a core pillar of Chicago's financial sector identity.
