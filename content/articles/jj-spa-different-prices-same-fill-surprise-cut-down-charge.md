Price is one of the first things a client thinks about when she books an appointment, and one of the last things she wants to be surprised by at the counter. A recent review of JJ SPA comes from a customer who visited twice for a basic fill and felt the totals did not line up. Her account is a helpful reminder that clear, predictable pricing is part of the service itself, and that small add-ons can shape how a visit is remembered.

## Same Service, Different Totals

The customer reports that she went to the salon twice and ordered a basic fill each time, with nothing different requested between visits. Even so, she says she "got charged two different prices." For a returning client, this kind of difference can be confusing, especially when she believes the service was the same.

There can be good reasons for a total to change, such as a different amount of product, a repair, a removal, or a pricing update. The challenge is that the client may not know about any of these unless someone tells her. A quick explanation before the service begins, such as "A fill starts at this price, and here is what could change it," helps guests feel informed. A receipt that lists each item, including add-ons, makes it easy for clients to see exactly what they paid for.

## The Cut Down Charge

The reviewer also described being charged $5.00 for a "cut down." She noted that only a few weeks had passed since her previous appointment, so her nails were not especially long to begin with. From her point of view, the charge did not seem to match the work.

A cut down is a common add-on when nails need to be shortened before a fill or a new set, and many salons price it separately. The key is to let the client know in advance. A simple question at the start, such as "Would you like me to shorten the length today? It is a small additional charge," gives the guest a choice and avoids a surprise at checkout. When a client's nails are already at a comfortable length, a technician can use judgment and skip the extra fee, which often earns goodwill far greater than the amount saved.

## Making Prices Easy to Understand

Taken together, the two concerns point to a single theme: the guest wanted to know what to expect. A posted price list with clear descriptions, a short conversation at the start of each visit, and consistent practices from one technician to another can all help. When prices do change, a friendly note at the front desk or on the menu keeps regular clients from feeling caught off guard.

Teams can also consider a short check at the end of each appointment. Reviewing the total with the client before payment, and explaining any add-ons, takes only a moment and prevents misunderstandings that can lead to a lost client.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers three lessons for nail teams. First, keep pricing consistent for the same service, and explain any factors that can change the total before work begins. Second, introduce add-ons like a cut down ahead of time, offering them as a choice, and use judgment when the nails are already short. Third, provide itemized receipts and clear price lists so that guests always know what they are paying for.

Clients are generally happy to pay a fair price for good work. What they value most is not being surprised, and a salon that is open about its prices earns the kind of trust that keeps people coming back.
