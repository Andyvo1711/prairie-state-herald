Tyson Foods announced on August 13 that it is closing its beef plant in Joslin, Illinois, and employees were told their job duties would end that day or the next, KWQC reported.

A letter sent to workers said some employees might be asked to keep certain operations running past August 14 and would be notified separately. Employees covered by the letter will be paid through October 12, 2026, according to the station.

## Scale of the loss

Illinois officials said about 2,500 union workers lost their jobs with the closure, KWQC reported. The plant sits in the Quad Cities region, and the state later described the layoffs as a serious blow to workers, families and farm communities.

Cars were seen leaving the plant roughly 30 minutes after the official announcement, the station reported. One longtime employee told KWQC she learned of the shutdown with no warning.

## Why Tyson says it happened

Gov. JB Pritzker later said soaring input costs and a historic cattle shortage drove the closure of the Joslin plant, along with Tyson plants in Utah and Washington State. In a state release in September, the Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity said the national cattle supply has fallen to its lowest level in roughly 75 years, prompting beef companies to consolidate production.

## The state steps in

Speaking at a campaign stop in Rock Island in late August, Pritzker said he called the head of the local UFCW union and Tyson's CEO to ask what could be done to reopen the plant, KWQC reported. Tyson's response, as he described it, was that the company wants to sell the plant as soon as it can find a buyer. The governor said Illinois would work to find one.

DCEO said it has mobilized rapid-response services with workforce providers across the Quad Cities to connect laid-off workers with resources and jobs.
