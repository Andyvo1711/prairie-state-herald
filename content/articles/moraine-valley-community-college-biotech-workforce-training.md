Moraine Valley Community College opened a new biomanufacturing training lab on its Palos Hills campus this fall, a facility designed to prepare technicians for entry-level positions at life sciences manufacturing plants across the Chicago suburbs.

The lab replicates equipment used in real cell and gene therapy manufacturing, including bioreactors and cleanroom-style workstations, giving students hands-on experience with processes that would otherwise require them to already hold an industry job to learn. College officials said the program was developed directly with input from several biotech manufacturers with facilities in the southwest suburbs.

"Biotech companies kept telling us the same thing: they had open technician positions, but candidates coming out of general biology programs didn't know how to actually run the equipment," said the director of Moraine Valley's workforce development division. "This lab exists so that gap closes before students ever apply for the job."

The eleven-week certificate program is designed to fit around existing work schedules, with cohorts running in both daytime and evening formats. Several partner companies have agreed to guarantee interviews for graduates who complete the certificate and pass a hands-on competency assessment at the end of the program.

Illinois has positioned its biotech manufacturing sector as a priority for state economic development in recent years, and community college officials say workforce training capacity has lagged behind the pace of new facility construction. Moraine Valley said it expects to train roughly 130 students annually once the program reaches full capacity.
