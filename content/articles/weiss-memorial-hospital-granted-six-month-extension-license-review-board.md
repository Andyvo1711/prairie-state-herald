The Illinois Health Facilities and Services Review Board on Tuesday gave the owner of Weiss Memorial Hospital six more months to resolve compliance and financial problems, even though the Uptown hospital has been closed for nearly a year, ABC7 Chicago reported.

The board had considered whether to let the hospital's license lapse for good. Instead, it voted to extend what is formally a temporary license suspension through February 8, 2027. According to ABC7, the board's staff report said members could have attached conditions to ensure accountability, but eight affirmative votes approved the extension without any.

Weiss's troubles began on June 18, 2025, during a severe Chicago heat wave, when its heating and air conditioning system failed and some emergency rooms reached 90 degrees. State regulators said the breakdown forced management to evacuate all inpatients. State and federal inspections then found critical problems involving nursing and emergency staffing and the condition of the building.

On August 8, 2025, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services revoked the hospital's reimbursement agreement, cutting off most of its revenue. Weiss locked its doors and ended all care, including emergency services, and state regulators issued the temporary suspension of its license.

At Tuesday's meeting, Joseph Hylak-Reinholtz, an attorney for owner Resilience Healthcare, said the company is appealing the federal revocation and fighting state efforts to recover more than $27 million in unpaid taxes and fees. He told the board the company wants to see how both matters turn out before deciding what to do with Weiss.

Resilience also shut down West Suburban Medical Center in Oak Park earlier this year, a closure that prompted a state inquiry into millions of dollars in public funds. ABC7 reported that, between the two hospitals, Resilience owes taxpayers at least $104.8 million in unpaid taxes, fees and state loans issued for repairs.

The company's chief executive, Dr. Manoj Prasad, is also in a legal battle over his own future at Resilience and negotiations with his business partner, who is the landlord of both facilities. Until those disputes are settled, the future of the two safety-net hospitals, which together once served thousands of patients a year and employed hundreds of staff, remains unclear.
