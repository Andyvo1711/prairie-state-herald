The Chicago Board of Education voted unanimously on Oct. 22 to approve a four-year, $64.8 million contract with Unite Here Local 1, which represents more than 1,800 CPS lunchroom workers, Chalkbeat Chicago reported.

The deal ends more than a year of negotiations. The district and union reached a tentative agreement Oct. 1, two months after the union requested fact-finding, one of the last steps before a possible strike. The contract raises minimum hourly pay from about $17 to $21 and adds raises tied to years of service. It is retroactive to June 2025, with a 4% cost-of-living raise next July and a 2.5% increase in 2028. The union's original goal for minimum pay was $24.

Cook Irma Garcia, a 24-year veteran of CPS lunchrooms, will go from $22 to $29 an hour. "When I saw that we won this contract, I cried," she said, according to Chalkbeat. The district says its current budget, which runs through June 30, 2027, covers this year's cost.

At the same meeting, the Chicago Teachers Union accused CPS of not doing enough to seek more state money before the legislature's veto session. The union said the district denied its request to let members lobby in Springfield. CPS spokesperson Mary Fergus said King will testify at an upcoming state budget hearing and that the district plans advocacy efforts around veto session days on Nov. 17 and Dec. 1.

The stakes are high. The board passed a $10 billion budget in July that assumes $150 million more from the state, an assumption made at the urging of the CTU and against the advice of CPS leaders. Gov. JB Pritzker has not signaled support for more funding beyond the $350 million increase lawmakers approved in May, and CPS is funded at 70% of what the state considers adequate. The district has not said where it would cut if needed.

King also announced a financial working group to address recurring budget crises. As of mid-September, CPS had rehired about 350 of roughly 1,000 employees eligible to return.
