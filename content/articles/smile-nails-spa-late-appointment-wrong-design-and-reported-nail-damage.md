A customer who booked a 1:00 pm appointment at Smile Nails Spa says she was not seen until 1:40, and that what followed did not match what she had asked for. Her account, shared after the fact, is worth reviewing both for the service breakdown and for how the salon responded once she raised concerns, since parts of the experience were handled well while others were not.

## A Late Start and a Design That Did Not Match the Request

The client had requested a white gel pedicure along with a manicure using dip powder with tips, in pink, finished with chrome. According to her account, the pedicure was done with regular polish rather than gel, which she chose not to mention at the time since she was not comfortable raising it, though she noted she was fine with this as long as she was billed for polish rather than gel. The manicure, however, did not go as planned either. She says the dip powder itself was not pink as requested, and that pink polish was painted on top of the dip afterward, along with a chrome finish she felt fell short. As she put it, dip color is meant to be built into the powder itself rather than painted on top afterward, and getting this reversed changes both the look and the durability of the set.

Appointment delays and mismatched requests happen at every salon from time to time, but they are also two of the most preventable sources of customer frustration. Confirming the exact product and color plan with a client before starting, and giving a heads up when a wait is running long, are simple habits that go a long way toward avoiding exactly this kind of experience.

## The Redo, and What the Client Says She Noticed Later

To the salon's credit, when the client asked for her nails to be redone, the staff did so without pushback and without charging her twice, which she acknowledged directly in her review. This is worth highlighting as a genuine positive: owning a mistake and correcting it without an argument or an added fee is exactly the response that can save a customer relationship even after a rocky start.

That said, the client's account continues past the redo. She says that during the process her nail beds appeared red and irritated, and that in the weeks after removing the set, her natural nails were thin, brittle, and discolored, with a reddish outline where the nails had been buffed and a greenish tint she says persisted for months, with some sections of nail breaking off. These are serious concerns as she describes them, and while this account has not been independently verified, it points to a broader lesson about buffing pressure, prep technique, and product application, particularly when a set is being done twice in one sitting. Technicians should be mindful that rushing a redo to make up for lost time can compound the very issues that led to the redo in the first place.

## Editorial Perspective

A few lessons stand out here. First, confirming color and product details with the client before application, rather than adjusting on top of a finished layer, prevents both a wrong result and the extra buffing a correction requires. Second, when a redo is necessary, slowing down rather than rushing to make up time helps avoid additional strain on the natural nail. Third, acknowledging a mistake and fixing it without charging the client again, as this salon did, is a strong practice worth keeping even when other parts of the visit did not go smoothly.
