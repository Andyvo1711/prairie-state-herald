Venture capital investment in Chicago's fintech sector reached its highest level in five years last quarter, according to data compiled by a regional technology trade group, driven largely by continued investor interest in trading technology and payments infrastructure startups.

The sector, long anchored by CME Group and the concentration of quantitative trading firms based in Chicago's Loop, has developed a growing ecosystem of companies spun out of the trading industry, and funding data shows investment increasingly concentrated in companies building infrastructure for institutional trading and payments processing.

"What's different about Chicago's fintech scene compared with somewhere like New York is how much of the talent traces directly back to the trading floor and quantitative finance world," said the trade group's chief economist, who compiled the funding report. "Investors have started to recognize that pipeline as a genuine competitive advantage rather than treating Chicago as a secondary fintech hub."

The report found that Chicago-based fintech companies raised significantly more in funding rounds compared with the prior year, even as overall national venture funding for early-stage fintech deals remained relatively flat. Several Chicago companies completed funding rounds exceeding $75 million during the period, a threshold rarely reached as recently as five years ago.

State economic development officials have pointed to the funding data as validation of continued efforts to position Chicago as a fintech hub distinct from New York, including tax incentives for financial technology companies that locate or expand within the state. Industry representatives said the sector's continued growth will depend partly on whether Chicago can retain talent that might otherwise be recruited to coastal fintech hubs.
