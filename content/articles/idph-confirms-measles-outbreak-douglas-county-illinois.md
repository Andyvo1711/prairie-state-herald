The Illinois Department of Public Health announced on September 2 that it is responding to a measles outbreak in Douglas County after confirming at least four linked cases, according to reports by WAND and KBSI.

IDPH defines an outbreak as three or more cases connected by a common source of exposure or another epidemiological link. The agency said it is working with local health departments and health care providers to identify where infectious patients may have exposed others, notify those people and offer vaccination to residents at high risk and others in the community.

Officials explained what can be done after a known exposure. Unvaccinated people age six months and older can receive the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine as post-exposure prophylaxis, but it must be given within 72 hours of first exposure. People at high risk who cannot be vaccinated, or who miss that window, may be eligible for an injection of immune globulin. That group includes infants, pregnant people and those with weakened immune systems.

Measles is a highly contagious viral illness that spreads through the air when an infected person coughs or sneezes, and through direct contact with nose or throat secretions.

IDPH Director Dr. Sameer Vohra said the state benefits from high vaccination rates overall, but some areas have lower rates that leave people at high risk. He said the department had been preparing for possible cases as measles activity continues nationally.

"Two doses of measles vaccine are 97% effective in preventing measles," Vohra said, urging residents to make sure they and their families are current on MMR and other age-appropriate immunizations. The first dose is typically given around age 1 and the second between ages 4 and 6, and the second can be given as soon as 28 days after the first.

IDPH also advised travelers to be vaccinated at least two weeks before international trips or visits to areas with measles outbreaks, and encouraged anyone without immunity to talk with a health care provider.
