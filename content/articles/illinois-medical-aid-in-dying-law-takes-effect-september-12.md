Illinois' medical aid in dying law took effect on September 12, making the state the first in the Midwest to allow terminally ill adults to obtain medication to end their lives, according to a legal analysis by the law firm Hinshaw & Culbertson.

The statute, formally the End-of-Life Options for Terminally Ill Patients Act and known as Deb's Law, was signed by Gov. JB Pritzker in December 2025 after a two-year legislative effort. Modeled on Oregon's 1997 law, it lets Illinois-licensed physicians prescribe medication that a qualified patient can take themselves.

To qualify, a patient must be an adult Illinois resident with a terminal illness expected to end in death within six months, with the mental capacity to make medical decisions and the ability to ingest the medication. An attending physician and a consulting physician must both confirm the diagnosis, prognosis and capacity. Age, disability or a mental health condition such as depression alone does not make someone eligible.

Only the patient can request the medication. The process requires an oral request, a written request on an IDPH form signed before two witnesses, and a second oral request at least five days after the first, unless death is expected within five days. Patients can rescind at any time. If either physician doubts capacity, the patient must be referred to a mental health professional.

Physicians must file a checklist form with the Illinois Department of Public Health within 30 days of prescribing. The death certificate must list the underlying illness as the cause of death rather than suicide or homicide.

Participation is voluntary. Individual clinicians may decline without discipline, and health care institutions may prohibit the practice by those acting on their behalf.

The law arrived amid litigation. On September 10, a federal judge declined to block it in a challenge brought by disability-rights groups, ruling the plaintiffs had not shown a concrete and imminent injury. Hinshaw advised hospitals, hospices, long-term care facilities and faith-based systems to review policies, staff training and documentation now.
