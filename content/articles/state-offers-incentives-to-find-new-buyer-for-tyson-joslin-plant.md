Illinois is putting state money behind the search for a new owner of the shuttered Tyson Foods beef plant in Joslin. Gov. JB Pritzker and the Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity (DCEO) announced on September 17 that resources from the Invest in Illinois Fund will be available to help attract potential buyers.

## The goal

The state wants to return the facility to productive use, support jobs and the Illinois beef industry, sustain economic activity in the Quad Cities region and add beef production capacity, according to the announcement. A notice of funding opportunity for qualified proposals to acquire the facility is being prepared.

DCEO pointed to the site's assets: large-scale infrastructure, an experienced workforce, access to agricultural supply chains and proximity to major transportation routes. Those could support continued food production or other industrial use under a new operator, the agency said.

## Background

Tyson announced the closure in August as part of a broader restructuring of its beef business, citing historic cattle shortages. The Joslin plant employed nearly 2,500 people, DCEO said. The department says it has delivered rapid-response services through workforce partners in the Quad Cities.

Pritzker said at the time that the Tyson CEO had told him the company wanted to sell the plant as soon as a buyer could be found, KWQC reported.

## Reaction

"The closure of the Tyson facility in Joslin has had a profound impact on workers, families and communities," Pritzker said, according to the release. Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton said the state would make sure whatever comes next works for the larger community.

Illinois Beef Association Executive Vice President Josh St. Peters said the state's help could facilitate a new operator moving into the plant. He noted the state has more than 14,000 family farms raising cattle and said the market needs to reopen.

No buyer has been named. The state has not said how much money will be available.
