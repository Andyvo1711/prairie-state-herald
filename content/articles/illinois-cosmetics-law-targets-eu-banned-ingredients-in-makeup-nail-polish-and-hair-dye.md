Illinois is about to regulate what goes into the bottles, compacts and tubes sold in its salons and stores. Gov. J.B. Pritzker has signed House Bill 3409, which creates the Chemicals in Cosmetic Products Act, according to an August 19, 2026 report from the Illinois Policy Institute.

## What the law does

Beginning July 1, 2028, businesses in Illinois "may not knowingly make, sell, deliver, hold or offer for sale" cosmetics that contain ingredients already prohibited in the European Union, the report says. The measure covers makeup, hair products, nail products, tanning products, soaps and lotions, and perfumes and colognes.

The statute is aimed at products rather than at a particular type of business, so salons that retail shampoos, polishes or styling products will be affected along with department stores and online sellers that ship to Illinois customers.

## Who has to change

The Illinois Policy Institute notes that a company would need to alter a product only if it contains one of the banned ingredients. Large multinational brands, it says, already formulate to European standards and are likely to treat the change as part of routine product development.

The report names one example: L'Oreal's Maybelline would need to replace a washable mascara sold in Illinois with the version it sells in Europe, because the current formula contains a banned ingredient.

## Concerns about smaller makers

The Institute, which is critical of Illinois regulation generally, argues that compliance costs could fall harder on small manufacturers than on global companies. It also points out that Illinois joins eight other states that have cosmetic ingredient laws going beyond the federal baseline.

The group's analysis does not address penalties or enforcement, and its report quotes no regulators or industry representatives, so questions about how the rules will be policed remain open.

## What salon owners can do now

With nearly two years before the law takes effect, there is time to review the product lines a salon or spa uses and sells. Owners may want to ask suppliers whether their formulas already meet European ingredient rules and whether any items are being reformulated for Illinois.

The law's start date of July 1, 2028, gives manufacturers and retailers a long runway, but the Illinois Policy Institute cautions that some products may need to be reformulated or pulled from Illinois shelves if they do not comply.
