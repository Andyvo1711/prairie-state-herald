Mayor Brandon Johnson announced on Tuesday, July 7, that Chicago is facing a projected $130 million budget shortfall halfway through 2026, according to FOX 32 Chicago.

Johnson said some of the revenue the City Council approved in December has not materialized. He pointed to a plan to sell city debt to a private debt buyer and a proposal to sell advertising space on city light poles and vehicles. No firms have expressed interest in buying ad spots on 3,000 light poles, city vehicles and bridge houses, he said, and the two proposals together total nearly $130 million.

## The mayor's argument

Johnson defended his approach, saying revenue sources that target large corporations and wealthier residents are bringing in more than expected. He said he recently spoke with Illinois House Speaker Emanuel "Chris" Welch about progressive revenue options and still sees a path for a millionaire's tax and a billionaire's tax. He also called for a permanent fund to deliver property tax relief.

## Aldermen dispute the framing

Several aldermen rejected the criticism. Ald. Brian Hopkins of the 2nd Ward said the administration is in charge of administering the new revenue sources and has "dropped the ball." Ald. Marty Quinn of the 13th Ward said the council had rescued the budget at the last minute last year, and he criticized Johnson's earlier push for a property tax increase.

A briefing document circulated by Ald. Samantha Nugent of the 39th Ward argues the shortfall stems from the administration not fully carrying out programs the council approved, and that revenue from advertising, augmented reality and debt sales remains at or near zero. The administration has disputed that, saying some assumptions in the council's plan were unrealistic.

## What comes next

Hopkins suggested the city may need spending cuts and a midyear correction that could include hundreds of millions of dollars in efficiencies. Johnson acknowledged that a midyear budget hearing the following week could prompt further action from the council before work begins on the 2027 budget.
