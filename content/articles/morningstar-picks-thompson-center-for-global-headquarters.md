Morningstar, the Chicago-founded investment research company, will move its global headquarters into the redeveloped James R. Thompson Center, the firm announced on September 30, according to Bisnow.

The company will occupy more than 275,000 square feet in the Loop landmark and share the building with Google as co-anchor tenant. Bisnow described the lease as the largest downtown Chicago office transaction of 2026 so far.

Morningstar plans to relocate in late 2028, after its lease at 22 W. Washington St. expires. More than 1,500 of its Chicago employees are expected to work in the new space, which is similar in size to the company's current footprint, Bisnow reported.

"We were founded in Chicago over four decades ago and are proud to continue building our future here," CEO Kunal Kapoor said, per Bisnow's account of the announcement.

## A pledge to the city

Alongside the lease, Morningstar committed $5 million over five years, beginning in 2027. According to Bisnow, $1 million will go to the Commercial Club Foundation and $4 million to targeted grants for local nonprofits.

## Google's timeline slips

The announcement follows news earlier in September that Google pushed back its own move-in at the Thompson Center to 2028. The tech company had previously targeted early 2027, CRE Daily reported on September 18.

Google, which agreed to buy the building from the state for $105 million in 2022, is expected to take at least 600,000 square feet of the 954,000-square-foot renovated tower, Bisnow said. Mike Miller, Google's Chicago site lead, said the pairing of the two companies points to a bright future for the next generation of Chicago talent.

## What it means for the Loop

The deal keeps one of the city's best-known financial employers downtown at a time when many companies have debated how much office space they need. For the Thompson Center, which sat largely empty for years after the state put it up for sale, a second marquee tenant adds momentum to the redevelopment.
