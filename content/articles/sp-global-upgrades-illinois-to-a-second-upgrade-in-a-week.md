S&P Global Ratings raised Illinois' general obligation debt to an "A" rating with a stable outlook on Sept. 1, Gov. JB Pritzker's office announced. It is the second upgrade in less than a week, following Moody's decision to move the state to A1.

According to the governor's office, the action is the 12th credit rating upgrade of the Pritzker era and gives Illinois its highest S&P rating in more than a decade. Capitol News Illinois reported it was the firm's first upgrade of the state since February 2023.

## Other bonds move up too

S&P also raised Build Illinois bonds and Metropolitan Pier and Exposition Authority bonds to A+, and moral obligation-supported debt to BBB, Capitol News Illinois reported.

## Why S&P acted

S&P cited the state's record of disciplined fiscal management and what it described as increased credibility in governance. The agency highlighted Illinois' eighth consecutive balanced budget, according to the governor's office, even after revenue losses tied to the federal H.R. 1 law passed in July 2025.

In its report, S&P pointed to the state's recent history of conservative budgeting and proactive management of the fiscal gap across consecutive budget cycles. It also said it believes Illinois can absorb challenges from federal changes to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program and Medicaid funding, as long as lawmakers respond in a timely way without giving up recent fiscal gains.

## Still at the bottom of the scale

The upgrade does not change Illinois' standing relative to other states. Capitol News Illinois reported that Illinois still has the lowest S&P rating in the country, sitting one notch behind New Jersey, Kentucky and Pennsylvania. The state's rating was five levels lower, at BBB-, just a decade ago, one step above junk status.

S&P said a downgrade is possible if revenue weakens and lawmakers fail to address budget pressures proactively. More upgrades could come if the state makes progress paying down unfunded pension liabilities and finds new sources of revenue.

## Political timing

With Election Day about two months away, the back-to-back upgrades give Pritzker more to point to on the campaign trail.
