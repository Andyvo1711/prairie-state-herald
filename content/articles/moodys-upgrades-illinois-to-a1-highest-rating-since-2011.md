Moody's Ratings upgraded Illinois' issuer and general obligation bond ratings to A1 with a stable outlook on Wednesday, giving the state its highest mark from the agency since 2011, according to Capitol News Illinois.

The move is the 11th upgrade by a major rating agency during Gov. JB Pritzker's time in office. Build Illinois sales tax bonds and Metropolitan Pier and Exposition Authority bonds also received higher ratings. A stronger rating lets the state borrow at lower interest rates, which can save taxpayers money over time.

## What the agency cited

Moody's pointed to balanced budgets and a conservative approach to spending. Capitol News Illinois reported that state spending outside health care, pensions and education has grown by less than 1 percent in each of the past two years. The agency also noted that reserves have grown, which it said gives Illinois flexibility to handle future budget pressure.

Pritzker said in a statement that the upgrade returns Illinois to its highest rating in nearly 15 years. The governor is seeking a third term, and the upgrade hands him a talking point for the campaign.

## The caveats

The analysis was not all positive. Moody's said unfunded pension liabilities and constitutional limits on changing benefits for current employees or altering the state's tax structure continue to hold the state back. The rainy day fund has grown to $2.5 billion, but Moody's noted that is still smaller than the reserves of states with higher ratings.

The agency also observed that Illinois' economy has grown more slowly than most other states over the past decade and could be hampered by minimal population growth.

Even after the upgrade, Illinois still carries the lowest Moody's rating of any state, Capitol News Illinois reported. That is a notable improvement from the stretch of 24 consecutive downgrades the state endured, including eight during the 2015-2017 budget impasse.

## What could move the rating

Moody's said further upgrades could follow if Illinois devotes more money than required to its pension systems while continuing to pass balanced budgets and build reserves. A downgrade could come if debt or other unfunded liabilities grow.

The stable outlook, the agency said, reflects an expectation that the state will keep up disciplined fiscal management and make gradual progress in reducing leverage. Moody's does not expect pension burdens or economic growth to improve enough to match higher-rated states in the near term.
