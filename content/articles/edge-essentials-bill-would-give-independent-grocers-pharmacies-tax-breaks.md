State economic development tools have mostly gone to large employers. A new proposal would extend them to the corner grocery and neighborhood drugstore. The Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity (DCEO) and the Illinois Retail Merchants Association (IRMA) announced October 28 their support for HB5828, dubbed EDGE Essentials.

## What the bill would do

The legislation would create a simplified package of tax incentives for independently owned grocery stores and pharmacies, with enhanced benefits for those in designated food or pharmacy deserts.

To qualify, a business must be independently owned with no more than four stores and plan a capital investment of at least $500,000. Grocers with more than 15 employees would need to invest at least $1 million and accept SNAP and WIC benefits.

Eligible businesses in food or pharmacy deserts could receive payroll withholding credits of up to 100% for new employees and 50% for retained ones. Those outside deserts could receive up to 75% and 25%. Participants would also be enrolled in the Enterprise Zone High Impact Business program, which includes a sales tax exemption on qualifying building materials.

## Industry voices

Rob Karr, IRMA's president and CEO, said grocers and pharmacies operate on very thin margins and are finding it harder to stay open. He thanked legislative sponsors Rep. Will Guzzardi and Sen. Doris Turner.

DCEO Director Kristin Richards said making incentives easier to reach for hiring and expansion would support small businesses and local economies.

Guzzardi, the House assistant majority leader, said incentives should not only help big corporations land big projects, and that EDGE Essentials would help independent stores provide affordable food and medicine in underserved areas.

## What happens next

The measure is a proposal and has not been enacted. The bill must still clear the General Assembly before any store could apply.
