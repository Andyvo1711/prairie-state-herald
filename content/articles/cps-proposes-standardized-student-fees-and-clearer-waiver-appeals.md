Chicago Public Schools wants to standardize the fees schools charge families for items ranging from sports uniforms to field trips and graduation, Chalkbeat Chicago reported on Oct. 8.

District leaders presented the proposal to the Board of Education's new finance and audit committee, which voted 7-0 to recommend it to the full 21-member board. The board's next meeting was set for Oct. 22.

Today, schools set their own fees at the start of each year after consulting Local School Councils and high school students. Under the plan, a district-level committee led by the head of the Office of Network Support would set standardized fees, though schools could request changes. Crystal Dorsey, executive director of school support, said the goal is a more equitable structure across schools. CPS is still gathering data and plans to seek feedback from parent leaders.

The district defines a school fee as a payment tied to a requirement for participating in a curricular or extracurricular program. It does not cover ordinary supplies such as pencils and notebooks.

The waiver changes would add students in temporary housing, who are protected by the federal McKinney-Vento Act, as state law requires. Currently, fees can be waived for students who qualify for free or reduced-price meals and for children of veterans or active-duty military with income at or below 200% of the poverty line. Principals may also waive fees in extenuating circumstances.

A new appeals process would give families 30 days to challenge a denied waiver through a form sent to the Office of Network Support, which would make the final call. Families would not have to pay while an appeal is pending.

Board Vice President Angel Velez, who chairs the committee, asked what share of students get waivers; Dorsey did not have the number. About 69% of CPS students come from low-income households. Velez acknowledged fees help schools offset costs but asked why have them if 70-80% of students could waive the fee.
