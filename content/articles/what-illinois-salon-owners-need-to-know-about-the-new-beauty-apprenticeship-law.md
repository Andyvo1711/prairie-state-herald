Illinois salons and barbershops will soon be able to train their own future licensees, but not just yet. A guide published August 6, 2026, by the New Age Spa Institute, a Des Plaines beauty school, walks through what House Bill 3460 requires and what remains undecided.

## The basics

Gov. J.B. Pritzker approved the bill, enacted as Public Act 104-0478, on June 26, 2026. It takes effect January 1, 2027, and creates an apprenticeship pathway to licensure in esthetics, cosmetology, nail technology, barbering and hair braiding. It does not replace school-based training.

## The hours

The guide lists the following practical apprenticeship hours, alongside the school route. Esthetician: 1,500 hours plus 90 hours of supplemental instruction, versus 750 at school. Cosmetologist or barber: 3,000 plus 180, versus 1,500. Nail technician: 700 plus 42, versus 350. Hair braider: 600 hours plus 36 hours of instruction.

The Illinois Policy Institute has noted that apprenticeships generally require more hours than school, but that apprentices must be paid at least the minimum wage.

## Obligations for salons

According to the school's summary of the statute, a salon is not automatically an apprenticeship sponsor; it must comply with the law and any approval, registration, documentation and reporting requirements set by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation. The supervising licensee needs at least three years of licensed experience or a teacher license, and participation is restricted based on disciplinary history.

Apprentices must be employed and paid at least Illinois minimum wage, and the agreement must provide for raises as the apprentice gains skill.

## What regulators have yet to decide

As of July 23, the date the guide says its information was current, the department had not adopted rules. Open questions include how salons become sponsors, how apprentices register and document hours, whether hours worked before official approval will count, and what supplemental instruction must cover. The guide advises salon owners not to assume early hours will be credited.

## Why it matters

Schooling can cost over $17,000 and take as long as a year, the Illinois Policy Institute reports. Supporters say the apprenticeship option helps low-income workers and those re-entering the workforce. Owners who want to participate should watch for the department's rules before taking on apprentices.
