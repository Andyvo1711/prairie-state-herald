Hyundai Translead said on Oct. 22 that it is expanding its planned manufacturing operations in Will County, raising its Illinois job commitment to 3,750 full-time positions and its investment to $600 million.

The semi-trailer maker originally announced in March that it would open two factories in the Joliet area with nearly 2,500 jobs and an investment of more than $450 million. The new numbers add 1,250 jobs and roughly $150 million. A third production shift accounts for the increase, according to the company's announcement distributed through Business Wire.

## Reaction

The governor's office described the commitment as the largest manufacturing job creation pledge in the state's recent history, FOX 32 Chicago reported. Gov. JB Pritzker said the 3,750 new jobs are "transformational for the region."

Hyundai Translead CEO Sean Kenney said the larger commitment will create more opportunities for working families, strengthen the company's manufacturing capabilities and improve its ability to serve customers. The company cited long-term confidence in Illinois, growing customer interest and a recovery in the transportation industry.

## Training investment

To support the project, Illinois is funding a new Manufacturing Training Academy at Joliet Junior College. FOX 32 reported that the academy will double the college's welding capacity and expand programs for industrial maintenance and advanced manufacturing technicians.

The facility builds on a state program launched earlier this fall that funds manufacturing training academies at community colleges across Illinois to help address skilled labor shortages.

## Background

In the March announcement, the governor's office said Hyundai Translead would use two sites in Will County, including a former Caterpillar heavy equipment site and the former Lion Electric site. The company, a subsidiary of Hyundai Motor Company based in San Diego, makes dry and refrigerated van trailers, flatbeds, truck bodies and dollies.

The state said at the time that companies in its EDGE tax credit program had committed 3,275 jobs in calendar 2026, more than the 2,900 committed through EDGE and the REV program in all of 2025.
