The Illinois State Board of Education held its first public hearing on Oct. 5 to gather input for next year's education budget, and Chalkbeat Chicago reported that most testimony focused on evidence-based funding, the formula that directs state money to K-12 districts with the greatest needs.

Speakers included an Albany Park bilingual teacher, a rural superintendent and Megan Accardo, a paraprofessional in Grayslake District 46. Requests ranged from about $500 million in new funding to a general call to fully fund schools. Total school funding this fiscal year exceeds $9.2 billion, and lawmakers have nearly every year committed to a $350 million increase.

Districts remain underfunded, and the state is on track to miss its goal of full funding by 2027. CPS funding adequacy slipped from 73% to just over 70% this school year, and more than 700 districts are farther from their targets than a year ago. Hal Woods of Kids First Chicago said the challenge "is no longer designing the right formula." Accardo argued the shortfall was deliberate.

Chicago's school board passed a summer budget assuming $150 million more from the state, a figure that will not be known until the November veto session.

Career and technical education also drew attention. Adam Seaney of the Galesburg Area Vocational Center asked for $20 million annually for facilities, equipment and staff retention.

Other requests included $46 million for afterschool programs, $1.8 million for a principal recruitment initiative, $5 million for students experiencing homelessness, $800,000 for agricultural education and $67 million for free school meals.

ISBE will use the feedback to develop recommendations for the governor and legislature in January. It scheduled an in-person session in Springfield on Oct. 21 and a virtual session on Nov. 5.
