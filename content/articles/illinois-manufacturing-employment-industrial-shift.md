Illinois's manufacturing base is retooling significantly for advanced production and automation, according to state labor department figures, reshaping manufacturing employment patterns across the state's industrial corridors, where heavy machinery and steel-adjacent manufacturing has long been a dominant employer.

Rockford and the southwest suburbs, home to a large concentration of machinery and metal fabrication suppliers, have seen some plants retool production lines for automated and robotics-assisted manufacturing, while other facilities focused on traditional labor-intensive production have faced slower demand and, in some cases, workforce reductions.

"Illinois's manufacturing base has always adapted to whatever the broader industrial economy needed, and this transition is just the latest version of that, though automation is a bigger shift than what we've seen before," said a state labor economist who tracks manufacturing employment trends. "Some of our manufacturers are ahead of this transition and hiring for new automation-related roles, and some are behind it and struggling."

State officials said they are monitoring the situation closely, noting that the employment picture varies significantly by county and by which specific manufacturing segment a given supplier serves. Economic development officials said the state has been working to attract advanced manufacturing and robotics companies directly, pointing to several recent large manufacturing announcements in the state as evidence of progress, though they acknowledged the transition will likely continue creating winners and losers among existing manufacturers for several more years.

Labor officials cautioned against reading too much into short-term county-level data, noting that Illinois's overall manufacturing employment has remained relatively stable even as the composition of that employment shifts. State economic development officials said they expect the retooling trend to continue as manufacturers accelerate automation investment.
